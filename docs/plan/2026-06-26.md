## What Is Driving The Market This Morning

**The primary catalyst is a global chip/memory sell-off.** Overnight, Samsung Electronics and SK
Hynix — two of the world's largest DRAM suppliers — plunged roughly 10% each on the Seoul Kospi,
triggering a brief trading suspension. The ripple hit U.S. chipmakers hard in premarket: INTC is
down more than 3%, NVDA is down roughly 1.4–1.7%. AMD and Micron are also lower. This is a
sector-specific risk-off move, not a general economy panic, but the semiconductor group is large
enough (NVDA alone is roughly 7.5% of SPY) to drag the entire index.

**The macro backdrop is unfriendly.** PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, recently hit a
31-month high (headline CPI at 4.2% year-over-year in May, core PCE at 3.3%). The Federal
Reserve — now led by Chair Kevin Warsh — has held rates steady at 3.50–3.75% and has explicitly
pushed out the timeline for any rate cuts. Higher-for-longer rates are a headwind for growth and
tech stocks. This is not a new story today, but it is the tide running against buyers.

**It is a Friday, end-of-week.** Position managers trim risk going into weekends. Volume can be
thin after the first hour, which makes momentum setups choppier and mean-reversion more common in
the afternoon. Do not overstay any winner today.

**Pfizer catalyst note.** PFE is gapping up roughly +2.9% on its own news (mixed Phase 3 lung
cancer trial data, CFO departure). This is a stock-specific event, partially decoupled from the
broad tape. See the Names section below.
