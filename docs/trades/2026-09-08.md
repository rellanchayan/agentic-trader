**Why it was the right call to pass:**
The R/R math did not work. A $4.30 target move on a stock already 8.9% up in a macro-headwind
environment (oil ~$100, yield 4.80%, geopolitical risk active) is not a realistic intraday
expectation, especially with a 1 PM Treasury auction introducing directional risk to yields and
the semiconductor sector. Forcing the trade to avoid a zero-trade day would have violated the
core principle of protecting the downside first.

**What it reveals:**
The two-tick rule creates a structural gap-risk: fast-moving names travel significantly between
the first and second confirming tick (64 minutes and $1.14 in this case). By the time the rule is
satisfied on a volatile gap day, the original entry price is gone. A supplemental check — "if
price has moved more than 0.5x ATR between Tick 1 and Tick 2, the opportunity has expired" —
would have surfaced this problem explicitly rather than requiring the R/R math to catch it late.
