## What Worked / What Did Not Work

**What worked:**

- **The plan anticipated zero trades explicitly and correctly.** The plan's section 7 stated:
  "The chance that all five ORB gates pass simultaneously in a 75-minute window on an FOMC
  announcement day is low. A zero-trade day today is the correct and expected outcome if the
  gates do not all clear." Zero trades occurred. The plan did not underestimate how difficult
  the session structure would be.

- **The 75-minute entry window is the right tool for FOMC Day.** Restricting ORB entries to
  9:45–11:00 AM ET means any FOMC-compressed session naturally produces a very short window for
  setups to develop. Institutions reduce directional exposure before a binary macro event —
  relative volume compresses, spreads widen, and the broad-tape unlock (NVDA AND SPY above VWAP)
  becomes harder to satisfy. The window design converts this structural compression into a formal
  gate rather than leaving room for subjective judgment.

- **The hard blackout from 1:30–2:30 PM ET is non-negotiable and worked as designed.** With
  no open positions entering the blackout, the FOMC announcement at 2:00 PM ET and the chair
  press conference at 2:30 PM ET posed zero execution risk. The system carried no directional
  exposure through a binary macro event. This is the correct outcome.

- **Semiconductor sector disarm held for the sixth consecutive session.** The disarm was
  triggered by two structural catalysts (Apple enabling developers to drop Intel Mac support;
  AI safety essay sector impact) and has been documented and consistently enforced every session
  since Sep 9. No INTC entry has been evaluated under this disarm. Six sessions of consistent
  enforcement without a lapse is the system operating correctly.

- **VWAP reclaim correctly disarmed all session.** The playbook rule — VWAP reclaim is off-table
  on MIXED/RANGE + active or imminent macro event — was enforced at every tick without ambiguity.
  No attempt was made to substitute a lower-quality setup to avoid a zero-trade day.

- **The post-FOMC elevated bar (3.0x rel-vol, ≤10 bp spread, 50% size) prevented any forced
  post-announcement entry.** After a Fed announcement, the tape can produce apparent trend
  moves that are actually whipsaw. The elevated post-FOMC bar requires unusual conviction and
  liquidity from the market before any entry is considered. No post-FOMC entry triggered.

**What did not work or is worth honest reflection:**

- **Six consecutive zero-trade days.** The streak now runs Sep 9 through Sep 16 — six sessions,
  zero trades. Every session in this streak has a documented, defensible structural reason:
  - Sep 9, 10, 11: Semiconductor sector disarm onset; RISK-OFF regime; pre-NFP suppression.
  - Sep 14: RISK-OFF; semiconductor disarm active; broad-tape unlock failing repeatedly.
  - Sep 15: RISK-OFF; pre-FOMC Day 1 vol compression; data cluster at 8:30 AM.

  No session in this streak had a setup that cleared all gates and was bypassed. The zero-trade
  outcomes have been correct on the merits. The account has preserved $999,465.31 through six
  consecutive difficult sessions. That is the gates doing their job.

  However, six sessions without a realized trade is a number that demands honest scrutiny. The
  question for tomorrow (Sep 17) is whether the post-FOMC environment produces a genuinely

  entries. If zero trades occur on Sep 17 with no major event and no FOMC/CPI/NFP overhang,
