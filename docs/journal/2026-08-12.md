## What Didn't Work

**The companion stop order was placed approximately one hour after the fill was confirmed.**
The fill was confirmed at the 11:41 AM tick. The companion stop-limit SELL (44 shares, stop
$220.30, limit $219.00, order_id 913ff6a5) was not placed until the 12:41 PM tick — a full
60-minute gap. The plan stated explicitly: "Place companion stop order with broker immediately
after entry fill. Do not consider the position managed until the stop order is confirmed." The
11:41 AM tick log itself acknowledged this: "Companion stop-limit SELL at $220.30 must be
placed next tick after fill confirmed via --reconcile."

For approximately one hour, the position had no broker-side stop protection. If NVDA had dropped
sharply in that window — toward the ORB low at $220.30 — the stop would not have fired
automatically. This is a meaningful gap in execution. The stop was confirmed at 12:41 PM and
was in place for the remainder of the session (order 913ff6a5 confirmed at the 13:40 tick).
The stop was never hit, so there was no realized harm. But the rule was broken.

**The breakout did not follow through.** The ORB was confirmed (price above $222.45) and all
gates passed at 10:42 AM ET — that part of the setup logic worked correctly. But NVDA had
already moved from its ORB high of $222.45 to $224.64 before entry; the 10:42 AM entry was
capturing a stock that had been running for nearly an hour. By the time the fill was confirmed
at $224.557, the immediate momentum had dissipated. The stock spent the remainder of the
session giving back most of the ORB break move.

**A late entry-window entry against a RISK-ON thesis that required follow-through.** The entry
was submitted 18 minutes before the 11:00 AM window close. For an ORB breakout to reach a
2.38x-risk target ($10.14/share above entry), NVDA needed to extend its move significantly
from $224.64. A stock that has already run +$2.19 above its ORB high (+0.98%) by the time
the order fires has less energy left for the run to target. The entry was rule-compliant, but
a late-window entry on a stock that has already moved meaningfully deserves an honest
acknowledgment that the odds of reaching a wide target are lower than at the start of the
window.

**The target at $234.70 was wide.** It required NVDA to move +$10.14 from entry in a single
session. NVDA's ATR is $7.57. A +$10.14 move is 1.34x the full-session ATR, from a late
entry, in a stock that had already deployed much of its early momentum. The tick log correctly
noted "Target $233.07" at 11:41 AM (the 2.0x-risk level) but the submitted file used $234.70.
Neither level was threatened. The same lesson from Aug 7 (NFLX: target too wide for a single
session) applies again with NVDA today.

**ORB setup cumulative expectancy is negative despite a 60% win rate.** The tuner reports 5 ORB
trades with 3 wins (-0.1994R expectancy overall). Today adds trade 6: a loss. When a setup wins
more often than it loses but still shows negative expectancy, it means the average loss is larger
than the average win. That asymmetry is what needs attention — not just the win rate.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **The companion stop must be placed at the broker on the very next tick after the fill is
   confirmed — not an hour later.** The 12:41 PM placement was 60 minutes after the 11:41 AM
   fill confirmation. During that window, the only protection was the EOD flatten schedule. That
   is not a stop. The procedure must be: fill confirmed at tick N, stop-limit order submitted at
   tick N+1. If the tick loop cannot reliably do this, it is a code-level bug to fix, not a
   planning issue to revisit.

2. **A late-window ORB entry on a stock that has already moved materially requires a more
   conservative target.** At 10:42 AM ET — 57 minutes after the ORB window opened and 18 minutes
   before it closed — NVDA had already moved +$2.19 above its ORB high. Entering with a $234.70
   target (requiring another +$10.14) against a stock that has already deployed its morning
   momentum is optimistic. For late-window entries, the target should be tightened to 1.5x risk
   or 1.0x ATR from entry, whichever is smaller, because the session time remaining and the
   stock's available energy are both reduced.

3. **ORB expectancy is negative despite a 60% win rate — investigate the loss/win size
   asymmetry.** Three wins and three losses (including today) with -0.1994R expectancy means the
   average loss is roughly 1.5x–2x the average win. The win rate is not the problem. The
   risk-reward structure is: we are cutting our winners at the flatten schedule before they reach
   their targets, and our losses are larger than what the wins recover. Either targets need to be
   achievable within a single session, or the position needs to be scaled down further so that
   losses stay closer to 0.25R and wins are allowed to run to 0.75R+ before close forces the exit.
