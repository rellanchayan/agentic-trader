minute condition. Today, premarket spread was 231.2 bp — five times wider than yesterday's

NFLX spread gate blocked the only realistic setup. A 231.2 bp premarket spread on a $72.66

at this price level. That condition is unlikely to self-correct next session. The 25 bp gate
is doing its job; the question is whether NFLX at current prices is a viable candidate for this
system at all given an ATR of $2.66 (3.7% daily range) combined with persistent wide spreads.

**The semiconductor watchlist concentration left the system structurally inactive for two
straight days.** NVDA and INTC make up two of four watchlist names. When the semiconductor
sector enters a multi-day rout — Samsung miss Monday, AI-plateau fears plus circular-financing
concerns Tuesday and Wednesday — both semiconductor names are disarmed simultaneously by the
same sector-level rule, and NFLX must carry the entire setup load alone. Today NFLX could not
carry it. The system has had zero candidates with a live entry gate for two consecutive sessions
not because conditions were universally bad, but because the watchlist lacks breadth. A broader
set of names across more sectors — energy, healthcare, industrials — would ensure that a chip
sell-off does not collapse the tradeable universe to a single name with a spread problem.

**The tick loop cadence failure is now nine consecutive sessions.** The premarket, the plan,
the disarm logic — all correct. The loop — absent or near-absent every day since July 13.
Today's tick status is not even confirmable, which makes it the worst-case instance of the
problem so far. Today's gates would have blocked all action regardless. Tomorrow's may not.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **Zero-trade days are correct when every gate is independently blocked — but only when that
   is true.** Today's zero is justified: NVDA and INTC had a sector disarm, NFLX had a spread
   that never cleared 25 bp, BAC had binary event risk. Forcing a trade into any of these
   conditions would have been the failure, not the zero. But tomorrow's picture may be different,
   and the loop's cadence failure will matter the moment any gate actually opens.

2. **NFLX's 231.2 bp premarket spread is not a premarket anomaly after two consecutive days.**
   Wide premarket spreads that do not normalize after the open suggest persistent thin institutional
   interest at the current price level, not a temporary condition. With ATR $2.66 (3.7% daily
   range) and now two sessions of spread failure, the playbook update should flag NFLX as a
   watchlist candidate for replacement. A large-cap communication or consumer-discretionary name
   that reliably trades below 10 bp intraday would provide the same sector diversification without
   the spread tax.

3. **Two semis plus one comm name plus one bank — that is not a diversified watchlist.** When
   the semiconductor sector has a multi-day rout, three of four candidates are disarmed and the
   system depends on a single name (NFLX) that has its own persistent problem. The postmarket-
   learn playbook update should add at least one non-tech, non-semi candidate — an energy,
   healthcare, or industrial large-cap with demonstrated intraday liquidity — so that a sector
   event does not collapse the tradeable universe to a single name two days in a row.

4. **Nine sessions without a functioning tick loop means the strategy has not been operational
   since July 13.** The 19-session no-trade streak (last trade July 10, 27 trading days into
   this account's history) is driven almost entirely by loop cadence failure. The account
   lifetime statistics — 11 trades, 36.4% win rate, avg win +0.559R, avg loss -1.197R — are
   based on fewer trades than a single good week should produce. Until the tick loop runs at
   2-minute cadence for a full session, no other improvement matters.
