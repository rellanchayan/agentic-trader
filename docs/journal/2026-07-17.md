## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. The premarket failure is the only problem that matters this week. Six missed premarket
   phases in 23 sessions is a ~26% failure rate on the first and most important step of
   every trading day. No amount of tick-loop sophistication compensates for arriving without
   a plan. The scheduler or runner that fires the premarket phase must be diagnosed, fixed,
   and confirmed reliable before Monday's open.

2. When the premarket phase is confirmed to have missed, the tick loop should log that
   explicitly and exit immediately rather than evaluating candidates it cannot legally act
   on. A tick at 14:40 ET that evaluates NFLX, confirms it looks good, and then rejects it
   on timing is wasted compute and generates a misleading audit trail — it looks like the
   system did work when the actual failure happened seven hours earlier at 9:28 AM ET.

3. A technically strong setup that cannot be entered (NFLX at 2.68x relative volume, 1.4 bp
   spread, ORB confirmed) represents a real opportunity cost. Across six premarket-missed
   days, some of those sessions likely had actionable setups in the 9:30–11:00 AM ORB
   window. Those are not recoverable. The running P&L has been flat at $999,747.04 since
   July 10; the missed-premarket problem is a direct contributor to that stagnation.
