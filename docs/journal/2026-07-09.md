## What Worked / What Didn't

**What worked:**

- Premarket ran cleanly for the second consecutive day. The plan was written before 9:30 AM
  ET, names were assessed with documented reasons, and the tick loop had a concrete checklist
  to evaluate against. The process failure of July 7 did not repeat.

- The spread gate caught INTC. A 209.5 bp spread on a +4.6% gap stock is a warning sign:
  the stock was moving fast and the market-makers were widening aggressively. Entering a
  LIMIT order at the mid when the spread is that wide is risky — slippage eats into the
  trade before it starts. The constitution's 25 bp gate exists for exactly this scenario.

- INTC's subsequent breakdown (below VWAP and below ORB low by 2:39 PM) vindicated the
  spread-gate rejection. The +4.6% premarket gap did not hold — it was a technical relief
  bounce in a stock with genuine fundamental headwinds (18A yield delays, AMD competition).
  Staying out protected the account.

- NVDA's "ambiguous" pass at 12:39 PM was correct. Barely above VWAP, exactly at the
  minimum volume threshold, no confirmed two-bar signal — the plan explicitly said to pass
  when ambiguous. The stock moved another +0.88% higher by 2:39 PM ($201.87 → $204.11)
  but with volume below minimum the entire time, meaning the signal quality never improved.
  A trade taken on the 12:39 ambiguous read would have had a poor risk profile.

- The regime call held intraday. The plan's geopolitical override condition (WTI surging
  above $77, or S&P 500 futures down more than 1% from open) did not fire. The session
  played out as MIXED — Cautious, as planned, with no emergency pivot needed.

- The account ended flat. No overnight exposure. Equity $999,662.81 preserved intact.

**What didn't work:**

- The tick loop ran late in the session. Both intraday log entries were made at 12:39 PM
  and 2:39 PM ET. The ORB/momentum window closes 90 minutes after the open (approximately
  11:00 AM ET). By the time the first tick logged anything, ORB entries were already blocked
  (189 minutes elapsed). It is not known from the available log whether earlier ticks fired
  without producing notable output, or whether the loop genuinely ran only twice. If the
  loop ran at 9:45 AM and 10:00 AM when INTC's spread was potentially closer to normal,
  those decisions are not recorded. This is a gap in the record.

- INTC's spread was 209.5 bp at 12:39 PM, which is unusual for a large-cap stock in the
  middle of the trading session. The plan correctly noted that premarket spreads were
  illiquid, but the expectation was that live-session spreads would be 1-5 bp. INTC's
  outsized spread mid-session reflects the stock's intraday volatility from its +4.6% gap
  open followed by a reversal. When a stock is already in reversal mode (below VWAP at
  12:39 PM), the wide spread is the market telling you the direction has shifted. This is
  useful information for future INTC (or similar high-gap bounce) planning.

- NVDA's relative volume never reached 1.2x minimum. Flat-gap stocks on sector-driven
  sessions tend to see volume concentrated at the open. If the conviction is not there at
  the open, it often is not there later. The plan correctly labeled NVDA secondary; the
  data confirms that secondary status was appropriate and the VWAP reclaim for a flat-gap
  name in a mixed regime rarely generates the volume confirmation needed.

- Over 17 trading days, the account has placed 9 trades with a 22.2% win rate (2 wins, 7
  losses). ORB setup expectancy is -0.721R; momentum expectancy is -0.975R. Today is the
  14th day of 17 with zero trades. The system is too conservative on entries, OR the
  setups themselves are not suited to current market conditions. Both possibilities need
  honest evaluation.

   transaction-cost problem. INTC's 209.5 bp spread at 12:39 PM, combined with the stock
   trading below VWAP, told the same story the chart told two hours later (breakdown below
   ORB low). When the spread gate fires on a name that was a strong premarket candidate, read
   it as a probable thesis failure rather than a timing issue to wait out.

2. The intraday log has only two entries today, both well into the afternoon, after the ORB
   window had closed. If the tick loop ran at 9:45 AM or 10:00 AM when INTC's spread may
   have been tighter and the setup may have been cleaner, those ticks are not in the record.
   Tomorrow's plan should confirm whether the tick loop is logging every evaluation or only
   notable ones — silent ticks hide information.

3. Over 17 days, 14 were no-trade sessions. That rate (82% idle) is worth naming honestly.
   It is not necessarily wrong — quality over quantity is the stated principle — but if the
   system is rejecting clean setups because its gating criteria are too strict, the idle rate
   becomes a bug, not a feature. Tomorrow's premarket should ask explicitly: if INTC had
   formed a clean two-bar VWAP reclaim at 10:00 AM with a 15 bp spread, would the system
   have generated an order? If yes, the tick timing is the issue. If no, re-examine the gates.
