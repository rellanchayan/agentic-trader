## What Worked / What Did Not Work

**What worked:**

- **Both entry setups cleared the guards correctly.** NVDA 2.35x relative volume with 0.9 bp
  spread; INTC 5.46x relative volume with 2.7 bp spread. Both satisfied the pre-9:45 AM gate,
  the broad-tape unlock, and the constitution checks. No rules were bent to manufacture entries.

- **The sector disarm lifted properly.** The six-session disarm on semiconductors was lifted at
  the first post-9:45 AM tick when both INTC and NVDA were confirmed above VWAP. The plan's
  condition was applied literally.

- **Capital limits were respected.** $9,864 in NVDA and $9,973.60 in INTC — both under the
  $10,000 per-trade limit. Total deployed $19,837.60 — well under the $50,000 daily limit.
  Two positions open — well under the 8-slot limit.

- **The loss-stop was never approached.** The day was above baseline from entry until late
  afternoon. No compulsive averaging down, no adding to losers.

**What did not work:**

- **The flatten system is broken.** This is the most serious finding of the day. The three
  scheduled flatten passes at 3:50, 3:53, and 3:56 PM ET never ran. The flatten phase only
  fired at 4:00 PM ET — too late by exactly one minute for DAY-order LIMIT sells to execute.
  Every flatten order was either immediately canceled by Alpaca (the 4:00 PM pass 2 orders,
  status: `canceled`) or submitted but will expire as the market was closed (the pass 3 orders,
  status: `submitted`, which will lapse). This is a scheduler failure that must be diagnosed
  and repaired before tomorrow's open.

- **NVDA's volume fade was not acted on.** By 2:40 PM, NVDA's relative volume had returned to
  1.01x — indistinguishable from background noise. The original entry signal was gone. The
  playbook should define an explicit exit rule for volume collapse below a threshold (e.g., 1.2x)
  on an existing position, independent of the stop price. The stop at $218.40 was never hit,
  but the entry thesis had evaporated hours before the close.

- **INTC's +$120 gain at 1:39 PM was not locked in.** At peak unrealized profit ($120.97 at
  1:39 PM), INTC was 56% of the way to the $115.07 target with volume still at 2.31x. A
  partial exit or a trailing stop adjustment at that point would have protected the gain. Instead
  the position faded all the way to -$41 unrealized by the close. There is no current playbook
  rule for trailing stops or partial exits intraday — this gap needs to be addressed.

- **Entry extension on INTC was too large.** Buying at $109.60 when the ORB high was $107.04
  means the entry was $2.56 (2.4%) above the breakout level. This is the kind of chase entry
  that often leads to poor outcomes even when the setup is right. The stop at $107.00 was set
  below the ORB high, giving $2.60 of risk on a $2.56 extension — the entire entry extension
  becomes pure extra risk with no structural support above the stop.

## Lessons for Tomorrow (2026-09-18, Friday)

1. **The flatten scheduler is the highest-priority fix before the open tomorrow.** The 3:50 PM
   pass must run automatically and reliably regardless of session state. A flatten failure is the
   worst possible outcome — it defeats the entire purpose of intraday trading and creates overnight
   risk in an account designed to hold zero positions overnight. Investigate why the flatten phase
   did not trigger at 3:50 PM and repair it before the next session opens.

2. **Volume fade below ~1.2x on an open position should trigger an exit review, not a hold.** By
   2:40 PM NVDA had decayed to 1.01x — the same level as a random stock with no catalyst. Holding
   through volume death while waiting for the flatten is passive to a fault. A mid-session exit
   rule keyed on volume fade needs to be added to the playbook, and the tick loop must evaluate it.

3. **Protect a gain above ~$80 with a trailing stop or partial exit.** At 1:39 PM the combined
   unrealized P&L was +$114.67. That is real money on a $1,000,000 paper account. Without a
   locking mechanism, it decayed to $7.44 by 3:40 PM and is now -$47.31. The playbook has no
   intraday gain-protection rule. Write one before the next trade is taken.
