## What Didn't Work

**The missing-plan HALT fired for the second consecutive session.** At 9:53 AM on August 14 —
exactly as at 9:40 AM on August 13 — the first tick of the day found `docs/plan/2026-08-14.md`
absent. The safety gate halted the tick correctly, but this is now a confirmed recurring pattern,
not a one-off incident. The premarket phase appears to write structural data files (`levels.json`,
`open_baseline.json`) before it finishes writing the narrative plan file, and the first tick
fires into that gap. Two consecutive days is a pattern that warrants a code-level fix, not just
logging.

**NVDA's relative volume never approached the 1.5x gate after the open.** At the first full
evaluation (10:50 AM), NVDA was at 1.09x rel vol. It fell steadily to 0.57x by 3:39 PM. This
means there was no point in the session where NVDA's volume confirmed a legitimate breakout setup.
On a Friday in a mixed macro environment (Retail Sales in-line, no strong beat), participation
was simply too low in the semiconductor complex.

**INTC's ORB-low break was sharp and fast.** INTC dropped $2.48 between 10:50 AM and 11:43 AM —
about $2.48 in 53 minutes on a stock trading around $103. That is a 2.4% intraday drop after
failing to break out above its ORB high. A trader watching the tape would have seen the breakdown
coming (and the 3.8 bp spread at 10:50 AM was already tighter than normal, suggesting selling
pressure). The disarm at 11:43 AM was correct but the decline was already done.

**The INTC spread blowout pattern continues unresolved.** This is the third documented session
where INTC's spread widens dramatically intraday (to 51.6 bp on this day). The pattern is
consistent enough to justify a permanent rule: INTC's spread gate should be checked at two
points — at the potential entry time AND at a secondary check 30 minutes later. If the spread
has widened materially from entry evaluation, the disarm should be retroactively noted. The
current playbook disarms INTC on the first blowout; the spread history suggests INTC should
carry a "structural spread risk" flag permanently.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **Fix the premarket-to-first-tick timing race before Monday's open.** Two consecutive days
   of 9:40–9:53 AM HALT on a missing plan file is a confirmed pattern, not a fluke. The fix is
   straightforward: `code/preflight.py` should treat a missing plan file as a retryable wait
   (e.g., up to 90 seconds after market open) before escalating to a full HALT, or the premarket
   phase should write a small sentinel file (`state/plan_ready`) only after the narrative plan
   file has been flushed to disk, and the first tick should block on that sentinel. Either
   approach eliminates the race condition.

2. **When both semiconductor primary and secondary candidates break below their ORB lows by
   11:43 AM, the session is over — do not look for late-day entries in SPY as a consolation
   trade.** Today's log shows SPY was checked at every subsequent tick even after the sector
   context turned clearly bearish. SPY below VWAP all afternoon on a day when both NVDA and INTC
   were making new intraday lows offered no edge. Confirm in the PLAYBOOK: if both semis break
   their ORB lows, the tertiary SPY candidate is automatically disarmed for the remainder of
   the session.

3. **Add a permanent "structural spread risk" flag to INTC in the PLAYBOOK.** Three documented
   intraday spread blowouts on INTC is enough evidence to treat INTC as a structurally risky
   spread name. The flag should require a spread re-check 30 minutes after any INTC entry
   evaluation, and any spread above 20 bp at that secondary check should trigger an immediate
   disarm — even if the position looked clean at first evaluation. For now, if INTC appears on
   next week's watchlist, the plan should include this explicit two-point spread check rule.
