## What Didn't Work

**The setup entered into the wrong part of the day for a trend trade.** VWAP reclaim is a trend
continuation setup — it works when a name is trending above VWAP and has momentum behind it. Entering
at 12:46 PM ET on a pre-NFP Thursday, with the 2:00 PM compression risk explicitly noted in the
plan, meant the trade had roughly 75 minutes of live momentum window before pre-NFP behavior took
over. That is a thin runway for a setup that typically needs 90–120 minutes to reach its target.

**The vwap_reclaim setup now has a 0% win rate in two all-time appearances.** Per the all-time
per-setup stats: vwap_reclaim has 2 trades, 0 wins, -0.368R expectancy. The ORB setup has 5 trades
with 3 wins and -0.199R expectancy. Neither has a positive expectancy, but the pattern — that VWAP
reclaim entries on mixed-regime days have not worked — deserves attention.

**SPY as the "backup only" setup still got traded.** The plan explicitly labeled SPY VWAP reclaim
"backup only" — it was the secondary candidate behind QQQ. No information is available to confirm
whether QQQ was evaluated and failed its gates before SPY was selected, or whether SPY was entered
because it was the first name to reach two-tick confirmation. Ideally the primary (QQQ) should
clear all gates and fail before the backup (SPY) is taken. If that sequence happened and the trade
was correctly a backup entry, fine. If SPY was taken opportunistically without confirming QQQ's
failure first, that is a sequencing question worth examining in tomorrow's plan.

**The afternoon drift confirmed the pre-NFP compression prediction.** The plan said rel-vol would
decline and ranges would compress from 2:00 PM as NFP pre-positioning began. That is exactly what
appears to have happened — SPY entered at 12:46 PM never made progress toward the $782.28 target
and was closed at roughly the same price level three hours later. The plan's risk warning was
correct and arguably should have weighed more against taking the trade in the first place.
