and the initial knee-jerk rally into rate-cut expectations was the kind of setup that can
reverse quickly if the interpretation shifts.

Three names were armed in priority order:

- **NVDA** — PRIMARY ORB breakout candidate. Armed at ORB high $223.10. Required a confirmed

- **AMZN** — SECONDARY ORB breakout candidate. Armed but subject to a live spread gate (≤15 bp

- **NFLX** — TERTIARY candidate. Conditional: ORB breakout or VWAP reclaim, but only if
  relative volume reached at least 1.5x.

**What actually happened:**

The priority cascade worked exactly as designed — from the primary down to the tertiary.

NVDA never broke its ORB high of $223.10 during the entry window. The plan said "if NVDA does
not break $223.10, it does not get a trade." NVDA was correctly disarmed without any order being
submitted.

AMZN was evaluated and disarmed by the live spread gate. At the moment of entry evaluation,
the spread measured 28.8 bp — above the 15 bp maximum. No order was submitted for AMZN. The
spread gate exists precisely to protect against paying a hidden cost that erodes any edge;
28.8 bp on a small-size trade is significant, and the disarm was mandatory and correct.

NFLX, the tertiary name, was the one that passed. By 9:54 AM ET it had broken above its ORB
high of $73.74, was trading above VWAP ($73.58), had spread of 2.7 bp (well within the gate),
and was showing relative volume of 1.56x — just above the 1.5x threshold. All entry gates
cleared. A LIMIT BUY was submitted at $73.87 at 9:55 AM ET and filled in full.

The trade never approached its $79.12 target. Price drifted in a narrow band above entry for
the remainder of the session, and the position was closed by the EOD flatten at 3:56 PM ET at
$74.17 — capturing +$0.30 per share (+0.345R) out of a theoretical +$5.25 per share (+6.0R)
target. The exit was at the flatten, not a stop-out and not a deliberate take-profit.

The tertiary becoming the day's only trade is not a surprise or a plan failure. The plan
explicitly stated: if NVDA and AMZN do not produce entries, NFLX is the next candidate. The
cascade ran in order and stopped at the first name that cleared all gates. That is exactly
what was designed.

## What Worked

high. AMZN was disarmed because its spread failed the 15 bp gate. NFLX cleared every gate and
became the trade. The system worked in the intended order: evaluate primary, disarm if criteria
not met, evaluate secondary, disarm if criteria not met, evaluate tertiary, enter if criteria met.
This is exactly the designed behavior.

**The spread gate on AMZN was a real protection.** A 28.8 bp spread means the round-trip cost
on a buy-and-sell would be roughly $28.80 per $10,000 deployed before any price movement is
captured. The gate correctly rejected this. A 2.7 bp spread on NFLX was genuinely acceptable.
Spreads differ by a factor of 10 on the same day across names — the gate is doing meaningful work.

**NFLX's fill quality was clean.** Both the entry (LIMIT BUY at $73.87, filled at $73.87) and
the exit (LIMIT SELL at $74.13, filled at $74.17) had zero adverse slippage. The exit filled
$0.04 better than the limit, which is the expected behavior for a liquid name approaching close.

**The EOD flatten executed on time.** The flatten pass ran at 3:56 PM ET (pass 1), submitted
the sell order, and filled completely. The account was flat before the 4:00 PM ET close. No
positions were held overnight.

**The infrastructure ran from start to finish.** For the first time in five sessions (August

fired, gates were evaluated, an order was submitted, the flatten ran, the reconcile completed,

## What Didn't Work

**The target was set too wide for a single-session trade.** The target of $79.12 implied a
gain of +$5.25 per share from the $73.87 entry. NFLX's ATR on this session was $2.08. A
+$5.25 move from entry represents roughly 2.5x the full-day ATR — practically speaking, this
cannot happen inside a single regular session under ordinary conditions. The stock would need
to move more than its entire expected daily range just to reach the target, and there would
need to be a strong directional catalyst to sustain that move.

As a result, the stated R/R of 6:1 was a theoretical construct that the market could not
deliver in one session. The actual exit at +$0.30 per share (+0.345R) captured 5.75% of the
target gain. The gap between the plan's stated objective and the realistic intraday expectation
for a $2.08-ATR stock trading in a "fragile, cautious risk-on" environment was very large.

This is worth examining directly: if the system is designed to hold through EOD for targets
that a single session cannot reach, then either the targets need to be sized relative to the
single-session ATR, or the system needs a mechanism for taking partial profits when the trade
moves a meaningful fraction of ATR in-session. Neither existed today.

**0.345R is thin for a setup that required correct timing, gate-checking, and full position
sizing.** The system correctly identified the setup, correctly ran the cascade, correctly
avoided two bad entries, and correctly filled a limit order. For that operational effort,
the return was +0.345R — less than half of one unit of risk. That is not catastrophic, and
it is a win by the strict definition, but it reflects a target structure that leaves almost
all of the theoretical reward on the table by design (the flatten schedule) rather than by
market behavior.

**ORB setup historical record remains thin and early-evidence-negative.** Before today, the
ORB setup record showed 1 win and 3 losses with -0.36R expectancy. Today adds a win, moving
the record to approximately 2 wins and 3 losses. The expectancy has improved but remains
negative on a small sample. Five trades over many sessions is not a sufficient sample to
confirm edge. The setup needs more data before conclusions can be drawn.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **Set targets that a single session can actually reach.** A $79.12 target on a $2.08-ATR
   stock in a neutral tape is not a real objective for a bot that flattens at 3:55 PM. For
   ORB setups, the in-session target should be sized at 1.5–2.0x the per-share risk, or roughly
   0.75–1.0x ATR — not 2.5x ATR. A tighter, achievable target changes the R/R math to something
   the session can actually deliver.

2. **The priority cascade is the right structure — keep it and document it clearly each morning.**
   NVDA primary, AMZN secondary, NFLX tertiary is exactly how a gated watchlist should work.
   Today it produced a clean result: two disarms at higher priority and one clean entry at
   tertiary. The morning plan should always write the cascade explicitly so the tick loop
   and the journal can both trace which disarms fired and why.

3. **Infrastructure reliability is still the most important metric, not P&L.** Four consecutive
   broken sessions followed by one clean session is not a trend reversal. It is one data point.
   Before Monday's open, verify that the premarket scheduler is configured to fire reliably and
   that the tick loop cadence is confirmed working. A $40.50 profit is meaningless if the next
   four sessions break again.
