At 10:39 AM, AAPL was last at $305.64. The ORB high was $311.77. The spread was 89.7 bp.
VWAP was $306.57; price was below VWAP (above_vwap=false). Relative volume was 1.98x.

Two simultaneous disqualifying conditions were present:

1. **Spread gate failure.** 89.7 bp against a maximum of 15 bp. The spread alone was enough

   $308.73. The RISK-ON regime call was for names gapping or trending up from Friday — AAPL

Additionally, price was $6.13 below the ORB high of $311.77, so even if the spread had been
acceptable, there was no ORB breakout occurring. AAPL was disqualified for the remainder of
the day.

but the disqualification from Tick 1 remained in effect, and the entry window had expired in
any case. No action was possible or correct.

at 10:39 AM, it would have been paying 89.7 bp to enter into a name that was already below

$1.22 below the ORB high is not a breakout. The plan requires the price to actually cross
above the trigger, not to approach it. AMZN never crossed. The rule held and no premature
entry was made.

**The account ended flat.** `flatten --verify` confirmed zero positions. No overnight risk.

**No rule was broken.** On a day with no trades, it is easy to satisfy every constraint, but

## What Didn't Work

**The first tick at 10:39 AM is 69 minutes after the open.** For ORB setups, the most
important period is roughly 9:45–10:30 AM — the first 45–75 minutes of active trading after
the opening range forms. On today's tape, AMZN may have been closer to its ORB high of $286.90
at some point between 9:30 and 10:39 AM. We have no visibility into that window. The tick
loop produced zero observations during the period most likely to generate valid ORB entries.

This is an observability gap, not a rule violation. The rules were applied correctly to the
data that existed. But the data itself was sparse. If AMZN briefly traded at $287.00 at
10:00 AM with a tight spread, we would not know, and could not have acted.

**NFLX's VWAP reclaim happened after the window closed.** At 11:40 AM, NFLX was above VWAP.
At 10:39 AM it was not. The reclaim — if it was one — occurred in the 60-minute gap between
ticks. Again, this is an observability problem: the reclaim cannot be confirmed as a clean
signal with a single observation 40 minutes after the window expired.

**Three setups, zero triggers.** In a RISK-ON macro environment with 85% earnings beat rates
and strong futures, it is reasonable to expect at least one of three armed setups to produce
a valid entry during the morning window. The fact that none triggered is not a discipline
failure — the gates are doing their job — but it does highlight that the morning window was
not well-observed.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **The spread gate earns its keep, but only on the ticks that run.** AAPL's 89.7 bp spread
   was caught and stopped correctly. The problem is that the gate can only protect against a
   bad trade it can see. If AMZN had a $287.10 print with a 3 bp spread at 10:05 AM, no tick
   was present to catch it and act. Earlier tick coverage in the 9:45–10:30 AM window is the
   most important gap to close.

2. **AMZN was one dollar away from a valid entry, and we had no data between 9:30 and 10:39 AM.**
   On a day with strong fundamentals and correct direction, the setup was sound. The price
   simply never touched the trigger during an observed tick. That is acceptable — the rule is
   to wait for confirmation, not to chase. But a system that observes only 2 ticks in a 6-hour
   session is flying partially blind during the window that matters most.

3. **A flat day in a RISK-ON environment is not automatically a bad outcome.** No trade was
   taken because no trade met all criteria. That is the system working correctly. The risk
   is always that "doing nothing" becomes comfortable. The standard is not "were any trades
   taken" but "were the entry gates correct given the data?" On today's data, they were.
