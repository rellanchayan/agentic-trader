## What Worked

**The plan's pre-session hygiene was correct.** INTC and PFE were disarmed before the
open with specific, documentable reasons. Disarming at the plan stage rather than
evaluating and rejecting at tick time is more efficient and reduces the risk of a gate
check failing under time pressure. Intel's $15B offering was a known event before
9:30 AM; PFE's ATR was visible in the screener data. Neither name belonged on the
active watchlist today.

**Target sizing discipline was built into the plan.** The Monday plan explicitly
corrected the 2026-08-07 mistake: "do not use 2.5x ATR as the target." For NFLX,
the plan specified 1.5–2.0x per-share risk (~$1.50–$2.00 above entry) rather than
the $5.25 target that was structurally unreachable in a single session. Whether or
not NFLX set up, the plan had the right target framework in place.

**The flatten phase ran clean.** Fifteen seconds past 3:57 PM ET, the flatten pass
executed: 0 positions attempted, 0 submissions, is_flat=true. The account was flat
before the 4:00 PM close. This is the correct behavior.

**A no-trade day on a pre-CPI MIXED tape is a defensible outcome.** The plan stated
explicitly: "If no name passes all gates by 11:00 AM ET, take zero trades and document
it. A missed trade costs nothing; a bad trade costs money." Not entering a trade on a
MIXED tape the Monday before a major CPI report is not a failure of discipline. Forcing
entries to generate activity would be the failure.

## What Didn't Work

**We do not have per-tick gate records in this journal entry.** The summary file confirms
the outcome (zero trades) but does not expose the per-tick evaluation data — which names
were evaluated at which ticks, what the spread readings were, what the rel_vol was at
each evaluation. The 2026-08-05 journal was able to document eight ticks with specific
spread and rel_vol readings for each name because the tick log was available. That level
of post-session transparency is what the journal should contain. Today's entry cannot
reproduce it. This is a documentation gap, not a trading failure, but it means the
journal is less useful for calibrating thresholds going forward.

**AMZN's opening spread pattern is a persistent unresolved problem.** AMZN has now
appeared as the PRIMARY candidate on multiple consecutive sessions and has either
confirmed (2026-08-07: 28.8 bp) or is plausibly likely to have confirmed the same pattern
today. On 2026-08-05 it was 153 bp at 9:40 AM on a 5.3 bp premarket. On 2026-08-07 it
was 28.8 bp at evaluation on a 4.1 bp premarket. The premarket spread on AMZN is not a
reliable predictor of the opening spread. If this pattern repeated today (4.4 bp premarket,
wide opening spread), AMZN was correctly disarmed — but placing it as PRIMARY again on
Tuesday should come with the explicit acknowledgment that its opening spread is a
near-certain gate failure.

**Quiet Monday before CPI is a thin-opportunity environment by design.** Three of the
four armed names (AMZN, AAPL, NFLX) require rel_vol >1.5x to trigger an entry. On a
pre-CPI Monday with no fresh catalyst, institutional volume tends to compress: large
players are not positioning aggressively before a binary macro event. The watchlist setup
that requires 1.5x relative volume in large-cap names on a structurally quiet day will
produce zero entries on many such days. That is the gate working as designed — but it
also means Mondays before major macro prints are structurally low-probability sessions
for this system.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **CPI Wednesday August 13 is the dominant decision point — trade Tuesday conservatively.**
   The plan for Tuesday should explicitly weigh whether any entry should be taken at all,
   given that CPI lands 18 hours later. A position taken Tuesday morning and held through
   EOD is closed before CPI, which is fine. But aggressive size or a wide target on Tuesday
   sets up a scenario where the flatten happens at an awkward time relative to the CPI
   announcement the following morning. Smaller size and tighter targets are the correct
   calibration for the session immediately before a major macro print.

2. **Stop treating AMZN as PRIMARY until its opening spread behavior is understood.**
   AMZN's premarket spread has been acceptable on multiple consecutive sessions (4.4 bp
   today, 4.1 bp on Aug 7, 5.3 bp on Aug 5). Its live opening spread has been
   unacceptable on every session where it was measured: 28.8 bp (Aug 7), 153 bp (Aug 5).
   The plan has documented this pattern three times now. Either move AMZN to secondary
   behind a name with more reliable opening liquidity, or mark it explicitly in the plan
   as "assume spread gate will fail; evaluate only if opening spread has been confirmed
   below 15 bp at the first tick."

3. **Quiet Mondays before major CPI prints are low-probability sessions — plan accordingly
   and do not interpret zero trades as system failure.** A MIXED/RANGE tape the day before
   a pre-CPI holding period will produce compressed volume across large-cap names.
   The 1.5x rel_vol gate will block entries on most names in that environment. That is
   the correct behavior. The lesson is not to lower the threshold — it is to set
   pre-session expectations honestly: on a pre-CPI Monday, the most likely outcome is
   zero entries, and that is fine.
