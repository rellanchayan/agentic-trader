## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. A late ORB entry (70 minutes in) mechanically limits how much of a 1R+ move can develop
   before the EOD flatten forces the exit. Today's 0.355R capture on a 1.75R setup was
   entirely a function of time, not price. Earlier entries — in the first 30–45 minutes of
   the ORB window — give the trade room to breathe and a realistic shot at the stated target.
   When the tick loop identifies a clean ORB breakout late in the window, size the target
   expectation accordingly: assume EOD exit, not target exit.

2. NFLX was the primary name and did not produce a trade. The tick log has no entry
   recording why NFLX was passed. For the second consecutive session, the primary watch-list
   name's live evaluation is not in the written record. The tick loop is producing results
   (the NVDA entry was clean) but it is not logging every evaluated name at every tick. A
   tick that evaluates NFLX and rejects it is as informative as one that generates an order
   — that rejection should appear in the intraday log. Fix the logging coverage before
   Monday.

3. Both fills today beat the submitted limit price (buy $0.34 below, sell $0.10 above).
   LIMIT+DAY orders on liquid large-caps with sub-2 bp spreads are filling at or better
   than the limit consistently. Do not erode this edge by submitting overly tight limits
   out of false precision. The limit should be set at a fair price for the setup — not
   priced to be the absolute best bid or offer — and the market is returning favorable
   fills reliably.
