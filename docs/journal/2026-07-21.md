## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **The premarket phase and the tick loop are separate failure modes.** Today proved this
   concretely: one worked, the other didn't. Fixing premarket scheduling is necessary but
   not sufficient. The tick loop trigger — whatever runs `bash code/run_phase.sh tick`
   every two minutes from 9:30 AM to 3:58 PM — must be diagnosed and confirmed working
   independently of the premarket trigger. Do not assume that because the plan was written,
   ticks will follow.

2. **Two consecutive dark tick sessions in a row is a reliability crisis, not a blip.**
   July 20 and July 21 both produced zero tick evaluations. Combined with earlier failures
   in July (July 17, July 13 had limited activity, July 10 was the last real trade), the
   picture is of a system that produces occasional bursts of activity separated by multi-day
   outages. This is not a day-trader; it is an account that trades when the scheduler
   happens to work. Until the scheduler is fixed and confirmed, the P&L record reflects
   system availability as much as trading skill.

3. **The INTC setup from today (July 21) should be studied regardless of the outage.**
   A 15.4% gap on real news (Apple foundry), with an ORB entry rule, a spread gate, and
   a two-day earnings buffer before July 23's Q2 report — that is the type of setup the
   playbook was designed for. When the tick loop is restored, compare what INTC actually
   did between 9:45 and 11:00 AM today against the plan's predicted behavior. If the setup
   would have worked, the cost of today's outage is concrete and nameable.
