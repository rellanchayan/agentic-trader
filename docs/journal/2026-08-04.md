## What Didn't Work

**The tick loop failed for the second consecutive day.** The morning entry window — 9:45 AM
to 11:00 AM, the 75 minutes most likely to produce a valid ORB entry — ran with zero tick
observations. This is not a market-related outcome. The market was open; the candidates were
armed; the plan was in place. The loop simply did not run.

**INTC's spread blew out 20x from premarket to the open.** The plan flagged INTC as the
primary candidate on the basis of a 6.4 bp premarket spread. That figure was meaningless by
the time the market opened. A 3.8% gap produces significant uncertainty about where the fair
price lies, and market makers widen spreads accordingly. For high-gap names, premarket spread
is not a reliable estimate of the opening spread. The relevant question is what the spread is
at 9:45 AM — and there was no tick to answer it.

**A sound RISK-ON plan produced zero entries for the second time this week.** The premarket
analysis was correct. Palantir +17% and broad tech strength is a genuine RISK-ON signal. The
candidate selection was appropriate. NFLX passed all entry gates at 9:40 AM. The failure was
entirely operational — the loop did not run during the window. Correct analysis that is never
executed is indistinguishable from no analysis.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. **Diagnose and fix the tick loop before Wednesday's open.** This is the second consecutive
   session where the loop fired once pre-window and then not again for nearly six hours. NFLX
   passed all gates at 9:40 AM and was flagged for evaluation at 9:45 AM — a tick that never
   came. Before Wednesday, verify the cron job, process manager, or whatever is responsible
   for triggering ticks at 2-minute intervals is running and healthy. Confirm it survives a
   single tick completion without exiting. Run a manual test if needed. This fix is more
   important than any trading decision.

2. **Premarket spread on a high-gap name is not the open spread.** INTC gapped +3.8% with a
   6.4 bp premarket spread and arrived at 9:40 AM with 131.6 bp. This pattern is common: when
   a name gaps significantly, market makers widen spreads at the open to absorb the gap
   uncertainty, then tighten over the following 10–30 minutes. For any name with a gap above
   roughly 2%, treat premarket spread as directional signal only, and evaluate actual
   tradeable spread no earlier than 9:45 AM.

3. **A flat day on a good-plan day is only acceptable if the gates fired correctly — and they
   did.** The three names that failed spread gates genuinely should not have been entered.
   NFLX at 9:40 AM genuinely needed one more tick before entry. The rules held. The honest
   lesson is that holding the rules correctly still produced zero trades because the loop
   stopped. Correct rules applied to too few observations cannot produce good results. The
   remedy is more observations, not looser rules.
