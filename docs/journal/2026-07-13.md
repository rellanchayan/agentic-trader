## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. A deliberate zero-trade day differs from a process failure, and the journal should make
   that distinction explicit in one line at the top. Today's silence was planned. July 7's
   silence was a process breakdown. Both produced $0 P&L but they are not the same event.
   The regime read and the explicit pre-stated expectation of a possible zero-trade day are
   what separate them — record that clearly so the pattern is legible across sessions.

2. NFLX intraday evaluations need to appear in the tick log regardless of outcome. The
   third consecutive journal noting the same logging gap is itself a signal: this is not
   an occasional miss but a systematic absence. Every tick in which a watch-list name is
   evaluated and rejected should write one line to the intraday log: ticker, time, gate
   that failed, and the gate value. That costs nothing and makes every no-trade decision
   auditable.

   directional clarity post-CPI.
