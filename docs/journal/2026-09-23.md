## What Worked / What Did Not Work

**What worked:**

- The HALT gate functioned as designed. The morning plan specified a binary precondition for
  entry. When that precondition was not cleared, no entries were made. This is exactly the
  right behavior when infrastructure reliability is in question.
- The account protection worked: zero drawdown, zero new risk, equity unchanged from yesterday's
  close at $999,641.65.
- The premarket routine ran and produced a plan. This is an improvement over September 22, when
  the premarket did not run at all. A plan existed today, even if the plan's outcome was no
  trading.
- Discipline around NVDA held: the stock was formally disarmed at the plan stage because of the
  spread and the violation history, not reconsidered mid-session.

**What did not work:**

- The flatten scheduler remains unconfirmed as a working, reliable mechanism. This is the fourth
  session in the streak affected by the flatten-scheduler problem (Sep 17, Sep 18, Sep 21, Sep 22
  carryover, Sep 23 halt). We have now spent a full week of trading sessions blocked or impaired
  by this single infrastructure bug.
- Zero revenue days accumulate. Standing aside is correct when the alternative is uncontrolled
  risk, but zero-trade days are not learning days. Every session with no entries is a session
  where no setup execution is practiced and no real-time judgment is exercised.
- Two conditionally armed setups (INTC VWAP reclaim, BAC VWAP reclaim) were identified and then
  never evaluated in live conditions because the HALT gate blocked entry entirely. Whether those
  setups would have worked is unknown.

## Lessons for Tomorrow (2026-09-24)

1. **Fix the flatten scheduler or document exactly why it cannot be fixed.** One week of impaired
   or blocked trading sessions is the cost so far. The next step must be either a confirmed fix
   (with a live test) or a written explanation of what the blocker is, so the problem can be
   escalated or worked around differently.

2. **A HALT day is not a free day.** When no entries are possible, use the session to review
   setups in real time and record what would have happened — paper-within-paper tracking. INTC and
   BAC VWAP reclaims were identified but never tracked through the session. That data is gone.
   Next time infrastructure is broken, track the setups anyway so the learning is not lost.

3. **Cautious lean in a MIXED/RANGE regime is correct, but it requires active monitoring.**
   The Sep 23 regime assessment (flat futures, elevated yield, sector rotation out of semis) was
   accurate at the open. Whether conditions changed during the session — whether a clean setup
   emerged after 10:30 AM — is unknown because no monitoring records exist. Build the habit of
   logging intraday regime changes even on halt days.
