## The 15-Session Pattern: What It Means

This deserves a direct statement rather than another hedged description.

A day-trading bot that has gone 15 consecutive sessions without a single order is one of two
things:

**Possibility 1: The gates are miscalibrated.** The rel-vol threshold, spread gates, R/R
minimum, or some combination of conditions almost never clears in the current market regime.
Every session, legitimate candidates are evaluated and rejected by gates that are set too high
for the actual intraday conditions. The system is functioning as designed, but designed wrong.
Fix: recalibrate the gates with real data on what typical rel vol, spreads, and ORB breakout
frequencies actually look like in July-August 2026 tape.

**Possibility 2: The tick loop is not generating orders even when conditions permit.** The
setup evaluations are running, the gates are passing, but something in the submission path is
not executing. No orders reach `state/pending_trades/`, no orders reach Alpaca, and no trace
is written because the zero-order path writes nothing. Fix: add gate-outcome logging on every
tick, whether or not an order is placed.

The honest answer is: **we cannot distinguish between these two possibilities without tick-level
logs.** The summary JSON for every zero-trade session records only `num_trades: 0`. What the
tick loop evaluated, what it rejected, what gates fired in which direction, what rel-vol reading
QQQ had at 9:47 AM — none of this is recoverable. The 15-session streak is a real problem.
The reason for it is unknown. That combination — real problem, unknown cause — is the situation
going into the weekend.

## Lessons for Monday, 2026-08-31

1. **The setup-mix review must happen before Monday's open, not after.** The playbook mandates
   a setup-mix review after eight or more consecutive sessions without a realized profit. That
   review is 15 sessions overdue. The specific diagnostic to complete: in how many of the 15
   blocked sessions did a qualifying candidate exist that was correctly filtered (zero trades
   was right) versus how many sessions had no qualifying signal at all? These are different
   failure modes and require different fixes. Without this review, Monday's plan will be written
   with the same calibration that has produced zero trades for three weeks.

2. **Tick-level gate logging is the most critical infrastructure change.** Every tick where the
   loop runs and evaluates candidates should write one line to a session log: ticker evaluated,
   rel vol reading, spread reading, gate outcome (pass/fail and why), and whether an order was
   generated. This log must exist even when zero orders are placed. Without it, zero-trade
   sessions are unauditable, and the distinction between "gates correctly blocked bad setups"
   and "system structurally cannot generate orders" cannot be made from the available data.

3. **Friday is structurally the hardest day to trade under this system's rules.** The 2:00 PM
   hard cutoff combined with a 10:15 AM Warsh entry restriction left a 45-minute live window.
   The playbook should explicitly note that on macro-event Fridays with a compressed window,
   the zero-trade outcome is expected — and should distinguish these sessions from full-day
   sessions like Thursday where QQQ and SPY were unconditionally armed from 9:45 AM. Conflating
   these two types of zero-trade sessions obscures the signal.
