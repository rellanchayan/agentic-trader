Reality was consistent with the plan's cautious posture. The specific blocking mechanism — R/R
math failing after the two-tick delay — was anticipated but not fully modeled. The plan did not
explicitly calculate the distance the price might travel between Tick 1 and Tick 2 confirmation.

## What Worked / What Did Not Work

**What worked:**

- The **two-tick VWAP confirmation rule** functioned as a correct filter. It prevented a
  poorly-timed entry on INTC's opening surge, which — given the R/R math — would have required
  holding through the 1 PM Treasury auction with an unrealistically large target. The rule is
  doing its job.

- **Post-holiday observation hold (no entries before 9:45 AM)** was respected. The 9:40 tick
  correctly waited. This is now a settled habit.

- **No-new-entries-after-3:30 PM cutoff** was respected cleanly. The 15:39 tick correctly noted
  the window was closed and recorded a final accounting of the session without any late forcing.

- **Relative volume skepticism** was warranted. The plan flagged that post-holiday vol readings
  might not represent real conviction, and indeed INTC's 5.65x opening reading was a compression
  artifact that decayed all day, not a sustained signal.

**What did not work:**

- The plan warned about INTC spread blowout based on prior history but failed to account for the
  possibility that the spread would be fine — so the planning energy was spent on the wrong risk.
  The actual blocker was R/R, not spread.

- The two-tick rule created a structural problem: any fast-moving stock will travel meaningfully
  between Tick 1 and Tick 2 (separated by roughly 64 minutes today). By the time the rule was
  satisfied, the stop/target math was broken. This is a design tension the program has not resolved.

## Lessons for Tomorrow (2026-09-09, Wednesday)

1. **The two-tick VWAP rule creates a time-gap problem on fast-moving names.** Between the first
   confirming tick and the second (64 minutes today), INTC moved $1.14. By the time the rule was
   satisfied, the stop/target math was broken. The rule is correct for macro-headwind sessions but
   needs a secondary check: if the price has moved more than 0.5x ATR between Tick 1 and Tick 2,
   the opportunity has passed and no entry should be made even if the rule is technically satisfied.

2. **Spread expectations should be formed from live data, not history.** The plan spent significant
   attention on INTC's spread blowout history (131 bp, 51 bp). The live spread today was 1.9 bp.
   Planning for the wrong risk drew attention away from the real risk — the R/R math.

3. **PPI is Thursday pre-market and CPI is Friday pre-market.** Wednesday is likely the last clean
   trading window of the week. If a high-quality setup emerges Wednesday morning, it deserves
   serious evaluation. But the negative expectancy across all setups (-0.19R ORB, -0.37R VWAP
   reclaim) means being selective is more important than being active. A clean skip is still the
   default unless the setup genuinely meets every gate.
