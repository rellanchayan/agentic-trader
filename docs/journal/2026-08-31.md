## What Didn't Work

**Sixteen consecutive sessions without a single realized P&L.** This is the primary fact
of the day. The streak extended by one from Friday.

The root cause for today's zero is documented and auditable for the first time. But the
documentation reveals a specific structural problem: **the only moment when QQQ had strong
relative volume (2.57x at 9:39 AM) fell inside the pre-PMI entry restriction.** The 9:45 AM
Chicago PMI forced a 9:50 AM entry cutoff. By the time the window opened, rel vol had dropped
to 1.18x and price was no longer positioned for a clean VWAP reclaim. The system missed its
one genuine opportunity of the day due to a scheduled data event that compressed the window.

This is not a gate miscalibration. The PMI restriction was correct — entering a breakout at
9:39 AM with a PMI print arriving in 6 minutes would have been reckless. But it does point
to a pattern worth naming: when a 10:00 AM or earlier data event exists, the effective entry
window may shrink to a period when volume has already normalized back toward baseline. The
system needs to recognize that sessions with pre-10:00 AM data events structurally reduce the
probability of a clean setup during the live window.

**NVDA is increasingly unreliable as a primary candidate.** In three of the last three sessions
where NVDA appeared on the screen, the spread gate fired at open. The premarket plan correctly
flagged this both today and on August 28. The system should reconsider whether NVDA belongs
on a daily watchlist or whether it should be treated as a setup-of-opportunity name only,
evaluated live rather than planned in advance.

## The 16-Session Pattern: What the Logs Now Show

The August 28 journal stated two possibilities for the streak:

**Possibility 1: Gates miscalibrated.** Conditions that should pass are being blocked.

**Possibility 2: Tick loop not generating orders even when conditions permit.** Something in
the submission path is silently failing.

Today's intraday log provides the first real evidence to distinguish between these hypotheses
for a single session. The answer for today is neither: **conditions genuinely did not qualify.**
Every name that had good rel vol was either inside a restricted window (QQQ at 9:39) or
disarmed by spread (NVDA). Every name inside the live window had rel vol below the gate.

This does not resolve the question for the other 15 sessions — those remain unlogged. But
the log-infrastructure fix now means that from today forward, every future zero-trade session
will have a documented reason. The systematic uncertainty about whether "zero trades" meant
"gates correctly blocked" or "system could not generate orders" should diminish starting now.

The remaining structural concern: rel-vol readings below 1.5x for all names during the
9:50-11:00 AM window is not a one-session anomaly. If this is the pattern on most sessions,
the 1.5x threshold may be set above what the current August 2026 tape typically produces.
Checking actual rel-vol readings at 10:00-10:30 AM across the last 10 sessions would answer
whether the gate is miscalibrated for this market regime. That diagnostic has not been run.

## Lessons for Tomorrow, 2026-09-01

1. **The rel-vol gate calibration diagnostic cannot be deferred.** The intraday log shows that
   during the 9:50-11:00 AM live window, QQQ rel vol was 1.18x and all other names were below
   1.5x. If this is representative of the August tape, the gate is set above typical conditions.
   Pull rel-vol readings from the last 10 session logs and calculate what the 50th percentile
   reading at 10:00-10:30 AM actually is. If it's routinely below 1.5x, the gate needs
   recalibration, not a workaround.

2. **Sessions with pre-10:00 AM data events structurally compress the entry window.** The
   Chicago PMI at 9:45 AM pushed the entry cutoff to 9:50 AM — but by 9:50 AM, the brief
   volatility spike that drove QQQ to 2.57x rel vol at 9:39 had already faded. The playbook
   should explicitly note: when a data event falls between 9:30-9:50 AM, the ORB reference
   level is contaminated and the best setup opportunity may come before the window opens. Plan
   for this structure, not against it.

3. **NVDA has failed the spread gate at the open on three of the last three evaluated sessions.**
   A name that structurally cannot clear the live spread gate is not a day-trading candidate —
   it is a watchlist item. Until NVDA demonstrates a stable sub-15 bp spread at the open on
   at least two consecutive non-earnings sessions, it should be categorized as evaluate-only,
   not as a primary setup candidate in the premarket plan.
