## The Trade

- **Entry:** BUY 41 shares, limit $240.95, filled at $240.72. Submitted 12:35 PM ET.
- **Exit:** SELL 41 shares, limit $239.15, filled at $239.15. Submitted 1:35 PM ET. Stop triggered.
- **Setup:** Momentum continuation. AMZN had confirmed an ORB breakout earlier in the session
  (ORB high $238.45, relative volume 1.6x), had been trending above VWAP ($239.11) all morning, and
  had pulled back from an intraday high above $241 to $240.62. The stated thesis was that the

- **Target:** $243.71. Reward-to-risk: 1.85x.
- **What happened:** Price fell through the VWAP stop at $239.11. The stop-exit was submitted at a
  limit of $239.15 and filled at exactly $239.15 — four cents above the stop level. The fill was
  clean, almost exactly at the stop. The exit reason recorded is: "Stop triggered. Price fell below
  VWAP stop at $239.11. ORB momentum thesis invalidated. Exiting to protect capital." That is correct.
- **R calculation:** Entry fill $240.721707. Exit fill $239.15. Loss per share: $1.5717. Risk per

  supposed to do.
- **Entry timing:** 12:35 PM ET. This is two hours and five minutes into the session. The ORB that
  the thesis references set at the open — the entry is entering a pullback in an ORB-continuation
  trend more than two hours after the breakout. That is not a fresh entry; it is a late-trend entry.
  Whether AMZN's momentum was still intact at that point is debatable. The VWAP stop was the correct
  test: if price cannot hold above VWAP, the trend is over.
- **Verdict:** The stop was placed correctly and honored correctly. The trade itself is a judgment
  call that turned out to be wrong. The momentum was not sustained into the afternoon.

  VWAP, the exit was submitted and filled cleanly at $239.15.
- The exit limit ($239.15) was set 4 cents above the stop ($239.11), which provided a small buffer
  to ensure a fill without chasing. It filled exactly at the limit.
- Position sizing was within rules: 41 shares at ~$240.72 = ~$9,869 deployed, under the $10,000
  single-trade cap.
- No market orders. Both legs were LIMIT + DAY.
- The account ended flat with zero positions overnight, confirmed by both the flatten-verify tool
  and the Alpaca API.
- The loss-stop did not fire. With the daily P&L at -$64.44 and the daily loss threshold at $3,000,
  there was no risk of triggering it today.
- 100% fill rate. Both orders filled in full.

**What didn't work:**

- No premarket plan was written. This is the sixth consecutive session without one. The tick loop
  is making intraday decisions without any pre-session framework.
- The entry was at 12:35 PM ET on a momentum setup tied to an ORB breakout that occurred at the
  open. Two hours of drift between the original breakout and the entry is a long time. Momentum
  setups entered in the middle of the day have weaker continuation probability than entries taken
  close to the original breakout.
- The trade lost. The setup's thesis (pullback in an uptrend above VWAP) was negated within one
  hour. AMZN did not hold VWAP. The broader picture — what was the market doing at 12:35 PM? Was
  SPY trending? Were we in a risk-off session? — is unknown because no morning plan was written.
- The AMZN momentum setup now has a single-trade history of -0.975R. That is one data point, not
  a pattern, but there is no positive expectancy to lean on in this setup category yet.

## Lessons for Tomorrow

1. Six sessions without a premarket plan is no longer a gap — it is the normal operating mode. That
   is broken. Before the tick loop is allowed to enter a trade, there must be a written plan in
   `docs/plan/<date>.md`. Fix the premarket automation or require the plan file as a gate.

2. Momentum continuation setups entered more than 90 minutes after the original breakout are
   late-trend entries. The edge in momentum is strongest when price is actively accelerating. A
   pullback to VWAP two hours after the ORB breakout is a different trade than a first-pullback
   entry 20 minutes in. Label the time elapsed from the original breakout and apply a stricter
   filter for entries beyond the 90-minute mark.

3. The stop was the one thing that worked perfectly today. The VWAP stop was logical, was placed
   before the entry, and was executed without hesitation when price crossed it. The loss was
   -0.975R — almost exactly -1R — which means the stop did its job. Protect that discipline. A
   clean -1R loss on a stop-out is not a failure of process; it is the process working.
