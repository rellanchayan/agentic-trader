**What worked:**

  regime read, a set of conditions for each name, and a defined trading window. This is the

  setups before the open was appropriate given the Iran news and FOMC risk. Calling it correctly
  in the plan meant the bot had no temptation to second-guess itself intraday.

- The FOMC minutes cut-off was enforced. Setting a hard 1:30 PM ET entry cut-off and planning
  to be flat before 1:45 PM ET was prudent risk management. FOMC minutes under a new chair with
  hawkish members is not a moment to hold intraday positions.

- The loss-stop flag was properly cleared at the open and was never triggered, because no capital
  was deployed.

- The account ended flat with cash equal to equity. No overnight exposure was created.

**What didn't work:**

- Nothing structural failed today. The process worked and the outcome was the one intended.

- At the margin, the AAPL watch conditions could have been defined more precisely. "SPY
  stabilizes before 11:00 AM ET" is a judgment call, not a measurable rule. If the session had
  been less clearly risk-off, ambiguity in what "stabilizes" means could have led to a
  premature entry. For future risk-off plans, the condition should specify a concrete criterion
  (e.g., "SPY reclaims its 5-minute VWAP and holds for two consecutive bars").

- Over the 16-day history, the account has placed 9 trades with a 22.2% win rate (2 wins, 7
  losses). ORB setup expectancy is -0.721R; momentum expectancy is -0.975R. Neither setup is
  currently profitable in the data we have. Today's suspension of both setups is consistent with
  the evidence, not just the macro narrative.
